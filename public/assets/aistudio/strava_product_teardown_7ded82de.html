01

Product Overview

1.1 What is Strava?

Strava is a fitness tracking and social networking platform that allows people to record, analyse and share physical activities.

Founded in 2009 by Michael Horvath and Mark Gainey, Strava was built around the idea of recreating the camaraderie and competition of team sports. Its positioning extends beyond running and cycling: it aims to serve people who engage in many forms of physical activity.

1.2 Vision and value proposition

Product ambition: Make physical activity more accessible, motivating and connected by bringing athletes together.

Strava supports a broad range of activity types and integrates with connected fitness devices. Its product direction also includes personalised training and AI-supported interpretation of activity data.

2.3 Key behavioural insights

1. People are motivated by social comparison

Seeing friends exercise creates social proof and can trigger FOMO. Users may feel motivated to participate rather than miss out.

2. People want recognition for their achievements

Completing a difficult run or cycling milestone can create a desire to share the accomplishment. Kudos and comments provide social reinforcement.

3. People want community without constant coordination

Friends cannot always exercise together. Strava allows them to participate independently while sharing progress asynchronously.

Research signal: Strava's Year in Sport reporting has highlighted the breadth of activity types and the growth of activities such as walking. These trends support examining Strava as a broader activity community, not only a platform for serious runners and cyclists.

Problem Identification

Based on the product's positioning and the behavioural analysis above, several underlying problems emerge.

ProblemWhy it matters
Inconsistent motivationIndividual fitness goals can become repetitive and difficult to sustain.
Lack of social accountabilityPeople can struggle to maintain habits without encouragement.
Coordination frictionFriends cannot consistently align their schedules.
Limited recognitionAchievements can feel less rewarding without social acknowledgement.
Fragmented progress trackingActivity data may be spread across devices and applications.
Competitive pressureConstant comparison can discourage beginners or encourage unhealthy behaviour.
Core problem statement How might we make physical activity a socially rewarding and sustainable habit without requiring people to exercise together or compete constantly?

Our key product insight

Motivation

FOMO and social comparison

Seeing others participate creates an incentive to get moving.

Connection

Asynchronous participation

Friends can stay connected through activities without coordinating schedules.

Recognition

Credible achievement sharing

Recorded activity data gives context to personal achievements, making them more informative than unsupported social media claims.

Credibility qualification: Strava provides structured activity evidence, not tamper-proof verification. GPS accuracy, connected-device data and manually entered activities can vary.

04

User Journey

4.1 Core user journey

Scenario: A recreational runner wants to build a consistent running habit.

Discovery and onboarding

Finds Strava through friends, running communities or app recommendations.

Potential friction: Understanding the product's value without an existing social network.

Connect and configure

Follows friends, joins clubs and connects a smartwatch or fitness device.

Potential friction: Device compatibility and setup complexity.

Record activity

Goes for a run and records GPS, distance, pace and other metrics.

Potential friction: GPS accuracy, battery consumption and recording reliability.

Share achievement

Reviews the activity, saves it and shares it with followers.

Potential friction: Privacy concerns and uncertainty about what information is visible.

Social reinforcement

Receives Kudos and comments from friends.

Potential friction: Limited engagement can reduce motivation.

Return and improve

Sees other activities, compares progress and participates in challenges.

Potential friction: Competitive pressure or repetitive experiences.

4.2 Behavioural feedback loop

See others exercising→ Feel motivated→ Complete an activity→ Share achievement→ Receive recognition↻

This is the central engagement loop that makes Strava more than an activity tracker. Social motivation can encourage an activity; the activity creates something to share; recognition can reinforce the desire to participate again.

05

Proposed Solutions

These are product opportunities intended to strengthen social motivation while making participation more inclusive and sustainable.

Primary opportunity

5.1 Async Crew Goals

Problem: Friends want to stay active together but cannot consistently coordinate schedules.

Proposed solution: Introduce recurring, small-group fitness goals where members contribute individually towards a shared target.

Example experience

  • Five friends create a weekly 40 km collective running goal.
  • Everyone runs whenever their schedule allows.
  • Each activity contributes to the group's progress.
  • Members receive updates as the group approaches milestones.
  • The group celebrates completing the target.

Design principle: Emphasise participation rather than ranking everyone by performance.

Strava already offers group challenges. This proposal is an evolution of that capability, focused on small, recurring groups and shared progress.

5.2 Context-rich Achievement Sharing

Problem: Conventional social media posts often lack context around fitness achievements.

Proposed solution: Create richer, customisable achievement cards that bring together:

  • Distance, pace and duration.
  • Route visualisation.
  • Personal records and milestones.
  • Activity source, such as GPS or a connected device.
  • Privacy controls for sensitive information.

The goal is to make achievements easier to understand and share while being transparent about the underlying data.

5.3 Healthy Motivation Controls

Problem: Social comparison can sometimes create pressure, especially for beginners.

Proposed solution: Introduce more personalised motivation settings.

  • Prioritise personal progress over competitive rankings.
  • Allow users to customise their social feed.
  • Celebrate consistency rather than only speed.
  • Encourage recovery and rest.
  • Provide private, noncompetitive challenges.

The goal is to make motivation sustainable rather than compulsive.

Priority: Async Crew Goals. It directly addresses coordination friction, builds on Strava's social network, encourages recurring participation and supports beginners as well as experienced athletes.

MVP scope

  • Private groups of 3–8 members.
  • Weekly collective activity goals.
  • Flexible individual participation.
  • Shared progress tracker.
  • Milestone notifications.
  • Weekly group recap.

What to leave out initially: Complex leaderboards and detailed competitive rankings. Validate the value of shared participation before adding more competition.

08

Experimentation

Primary experiment

Experiment 1: Async Crew Goals

Hypothesis: Users offered flexible group fitness goals will demonstrate higher activity consistency and retention than users relying exclusively on individual goals.

Control groupExisting individual goals.
Test groupAsync Crew Goals.
Duration4 weeks.
Primary metric4-week active retention.
Secondary metricActivities per member per week.
GuardrailsNegative feedback, muting and group abandonment.

Success criteria: A meaningful improvement in activity consistency and retention without an increase in negative social experiences.

Experiment 2: Achievement Sharing

Hypothesis: Richer activity-sharing experiences will increase meaningful social interactions.

  • A/B test standard activity sharing against custom achievement cards.
  • Measure Kudos, comments and shares.
  • Track whether users return to record another activity.
  • Monitor privacy-related complaints.

This helps establish whether better achievement presentation translates into sustained engagement rather than only more sharing.

9.2 Strategic reflection

1. Social context changes value

Tracking a run is useful. Sharing that run with friends and receiving recognition can make the experience more engaging.

2. Asynchronous experiences remove friction

People do not need to participate at the same time to feel part of a community.

3. Engagement needs boundaries

FOMO and competition can motivate people, but the product should avoid making exercise feel like an obligation or a race against everyone else.

Final product takeaway

Strava's strongest product advantage is its ability to connect individual physical activity with social motivation.

It transforms fitness from an isolated personal task into an ongoing community experience through social visibility, asynchronous participation, competition and recognition.

The bigger lesson: Great products do not always need to create entirely new behaviours. Sometimes, they make existing human motivations work together to reinforce a valuable habit.
